> **THE CLAIM:** Robots need 17,000 human-like tactile receptors to be useful.
> 
> **THE REALITY:** They do not. High-frequency force-torque sensors and 1 kHz haptic loops compensate for spatial density with superior temporal resolution. We did not need to replicate human skin; we just needed to stabilize the control loop.

> **THE CLAIM:** Articulated hands are too fragile for industrial deployment.
> 
> **THE REALITY:** Modern units house 25 actuators in the forearm, shielding sensitive electronics and using metallic tendons for high-force durability. The fragility argument died the moment we moved the motors.

> **THE REALITY:** ISO 25785-1 has replaced physical fences with dynamic virtual safety zones. Figure 02 has clocked over 1,250 incident-free hours at BMW without a single safety event.

### **DEEP SIGNAL ANALYSIS**

#### **1. The Infrastructure Hack**
Brooks argued that successful robots would eventually look nothing like humans. He ignored infrastructure inertia. The world is already built for us. Humanoids are **Plug-Compatible Sovereignty.** They use our stairs, our clearances, and our tools. You drop a 2026 unit into a 1970s machine shop in Chico and it just works.

Brooks bet on geometry and lost to scale. The rise of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has proven that scaling data solves the physical world. Generalization is no longer a "mathematical failure"; it is a function of compute.
